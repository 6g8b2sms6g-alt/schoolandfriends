To herself, ten years from now

Hi, Sara!!! I’m Sara from 2026, writing this letter for you, Sara, in 2036. Right now, I’m 17 years old, and I’m finishing my last year of school. It’s a strange feeling because I’m excited about everything that’s coming, but I’m also a little scared. Tell me, did everything go well in December? Did we graduate? Was that day as emotional as I imagine? I imagine you’re studying Law at UBA. Did we actually do it? Did we ever go volunteering in Africa, like we always dreamed? And did you never stop making music? Did we finally buy the Pioneer XDJ-1000? I hope that passion never disappeared, because it has always been one of the things that makes us happiest.

Did you find love? Are our friends still part of your life? Are Mom and Dad healthy and happy? Most importantly: are you proud of yourself? Never forget the little pieces of the person you were in 2026. Never lose your kindness, your curiosity, your dreams, or your desire to help others. I love you so much, and I’m really proud of you, no matter where life has taken you.

See you in ten years. Sara (2026)

Anecdotes and school trips

The moments that turned into the stories we still tell each other.

The one where Francisco fell in the river

School trip, Bariloche, 2022

A funny memory from high school is when we went to Bariloche in 2022. During an excursion, we were all standing near a river when our classmate Francisco suddenly slipped and fell into the water. We all started laughing because it was really funny, and Francisco was completely wet. Luckily, he wasn’t hurt, and it became one of the funniest moments of our trip.

The night Sadisley flooded the cabin

School trip, Mendoza

We went on a trip to Mendoza with the school. Everyone slept in cabins, and we chose one for four people. Everything was fine and we were comfortable in the cabin, until one night Sadisley decided to take her time in the shower. The whole ground floor flooded, and everything on the floor was drenched. We were all laughing while we cleaned it up, but Sadis ended up cleaning the most, since she was the one who caused the flood in the first place.

Our favourite people at school

Two people whose classrooms, and pep talks, we’ll remember long after the bell stops ringing.

Lula Antonucci

Primary school teacher and first-year preceptora

She was our teacher in primary school and our preceptora in the first year of secondary school. Thanks to her, we learned about computers, and laughed a lot too. We would describe her as cheerful, charismatic, and patient, always there when we need her. We can’t forget the smell of incense in the preceptoría, taking all the bad vibes out of the room. We’re lucky to have had her, because she was part of the most important part of our lives: school.

Jimena Tello

Preceptora

She is very important to us because she always helped when we had problems. Jimena is responsible, supportive, reliable, kind, and patient, listening carefully to everyone who needs advice or encouragement. She is someone who always makes us feel comfortable and also helps organise school activities, which makes our school a better place. We admire the way she treats every student fairly and with respect, and we’re grateful for her support.

Advice for freshmen

What we would tell the students who are just starting.

If we could give some advice to younger students, we would tell them to enjoy every moment of their school years, because time goes by faster than they think. When it comes to subjects and homework, try to stay organized, pay attention in class and don’t be scared of Felisa, she’s fine. Managing your time is important, but remember that you also need time to rest and enjoy yourself.

We would also tell them that friendship is one of the most important parts of school life. Make friends, meet new people and don’t be afraid to be yourself. However, don’t feel pressured to fit in or compare yourself.

Be open to new experiences, participate in school activities and take every opportunity to learn something new, but do not forget that challenges and mistakes are part of growing up, so learn from them instead of being too hard on yourself. Remember that you don’t have to be perfect or have the best grades all the time. The most important thing is to cherish the journey and make good memories. These years will become some of the memories you will carry with you forever.

Thanks to school for…

We would like to thank our school for being the place where we grew up, shared experiences, and learned the values that shape who we are today: the Marist values.

Being Marist means being part of a family with truly unique qualities. The kindness, support, and understanding that this school has given us are things we will carry with us throughout our lives. Being Marist means sharing, caring, and growing together.

Thanks to our school, we have discovered who we are and who we want to become. We are grateful for all the opportunities we had to spend time together outside the classroom, whether during school retreats or camps, where laughter and heartwarming hugs were always present.

We are taking away thousands of memories, friendships, conversations, and lessons that will continue to shape us in the future.

Thank you for everything.
